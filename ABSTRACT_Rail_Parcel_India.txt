ABSTRACT

Rail Parcel in India: Market Opportunity, Global Benchmarks and a Transformative Operating Model

Indian Railways has long anchored the nation’s logistics backbone through an extensive network, cost efficiency and a lower carbon footprint per tonne-kilometre. In 2025, rail held roughly 28% of India’s transport modal share and moved nearly 1.6 billion tonnes of bulk freight. That strength in bulk cargo now forms the platform for a next growth frontier: parcel and express logistics, where e-commerce, MSMEs and inter-city commerce are expanding demand for time-sensitive, smaller-lot shipments.

The existing rail parcel business already has national reach—87 parcel terminals, about 5.9 million tonnes of volume in FY2025-26, roughly 6% CAGR over three years, and approximately ₹2,100 crore in revenue. Yet parcels remain under 0.08% of overall rail freight, signalling shallow penetration relative to network potential. Against this base, the long-distance parcel and part-load opportunity is far larger. India’s road freight market is projected near USD 180 billion in 2026, of which PTL and Express together account for about USD 23 billion. Domestic air cargo is near USD 1 billion and rail parcel about USD 0.25 billion. The total addressable market for long-distance parcel and part-load is estimated at approximately USD 8.75 billion, with a serviceable addressable market for rail of about USD 5.5 billion after accounting for first-/last-mile and ultra-time-critical constraints.

Global practice shows that mature railway parcel systems treat rail as the middle-mile spine of a multimodal ecosystem. Europe operates a shared-ownership, hub-and-spoke model in which CEP players own the customer interface while railways provide scheduled long-haul capacity, supported by intermodal equipment and digital coordination. China’s centralised state-railway model integrates high-speed corridors, hubs and digital platforms with major logistics and e-commerce partners. Both benchmarks emphasise dedicated parcel trains, clear role separation and end-to-end visibility.

India’s current ecosystem still faces structural gaps: passenger-station–centric terminals in congested city cores, limited mechanisation, lane-based rather than network-based operations, multiple intermediate touchpoints, weak standardisation of unit load devices, incomplete barcoding and POD/DWS deployment, and historically limited organisational focus on parcel as a commercial product. Recent reforms—parcel and PCET leasing, Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals, Jan Sadharan Parcel (JPP) services, exploration of Vande Bharat Cargo concepts, and a dedicated tracking application—point in the right direction but remain insufficient without a full operating-model shift.

This report argues for a transformative posture: productise parcel as an ecosystem-led offering; concentrate volume at dedicated hubs aligned to industrial and consumption clusters; move hub-to-hub with minimal intermediate handling; and assign first-/last-mile work to logistics service providers. Clear accountability among logistics operators, terminal operators and the rail network—backed by shared digital rails—can convert today’s fragmented capacity into a competitive, reliable door-to-door middle-mile product and allow Indian Railways to capture a meaningful share of the growing long-haul PTL and express market.

Keywords: Indian Railways; rail parcel; express logistics; PTL; middle mile; hub-and-spoke; multimodal logistics; Gati Shakti; PCET; JPP.
