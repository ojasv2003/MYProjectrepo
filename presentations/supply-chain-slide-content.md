## SLIDE 2 — The Landscape

**Section label:** 01 — The landscape

**Title:**
From fixed networks to constant motion

**Supporting text:**
Efficiency-first globalization is giving way to adaptive, higher-cost networks. Trade policy, geopolitical risk, and technology are now embedded in the cost of doing business.

**Bullet / cards:**

1. **Resilience over lowest cost**
   Boards now treat continuity and optionality as competitive assets — not temporary insurance after a shock.

2. **Policy as standing cost**
   Tariffs and trade barriers are no longer a short-lived storm to wait out; they are structural inputs to sourcing math.

3. **Fragmented geography**
   Multi-country footprints — Mexico, Vietnam, India, and beyond — replace single-source dependence on China.

**Source note:**
KPMG Biannual Supply Chain Report (Mar 2026); DP World Global Trade Observatory 2026

## SLIDE 3 — Tariffs & Trade Wars

**Section label:** 02 — Tariffs & trade wars

**Title:**
Tariffs rewired sourcing — they did not end imports

**Key stats:**

| Stat | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| −28% | Drop in US imports from China in 2025 after renewed tariff escalation |
| +9% | Rise in US imports from the rest of the world in the same period |
| ~9% | China’s share of US imports by end-2025 (down from ~22% pre-2018) |

**Bullets:**
- Electronics shifted fast to India and Vietnam; apparel often did not — tariff differentials were too small to force a move.
- USMCA carve-outs remain a major mitigator; renegotiation risk could add several points to effective tariff rates.
- Most firms still raise prices and delay capital projects rather than fully reshore overnight.

**Source note:**
PIIE Working Paper 26-14; KPMG 2026 Tariff Survey; Kearney 2026 Reshoring Index

## SLIDE 4 — Wars & Geopolitics

**Section label:** 03 — Wars & geopolitics

**Title:**
Conflict risk is no longer episodic

**Quote / callout:**
Geopolitical risk indices in 2025 reached levels not seen since 2022 — among the highest since the early 2000s wars.

**Bullets:**
- Russia–Ukraine continues to scramble energy, grain, and industrial metals corridors.
- Middle East tensions keep insurance premiums and route choices volatile.
- Export controls and dual-use restrictions harden tech and critical-materials flows.

**Side cards:**

1. **Political distance ≠ supply safety**
   “Friendly” assembly hubs often still depend on Chinese inputs — friendshoring can relocate risk rather than eliminate it.

2. **Scenario planning is table stakes**
   Leaders model tariff, conflict, and chokepoint scenarios together — not as separate risk silos.

**Source note:**
KPMG Mar 2026; Economist Impact Trade in Transition 2025; Capgemini Reindustrialization 2025

## SLIDE 5 — Physical Disruption

**Section label:** 04 — Physical disruption

**Title:**
Chokepoints still set the tempo of trade

**Cards:**

1. **Red Sea / Suez**
   Attacks forced widespread Cape of Good Hope detours — adding roughly 7–12 days and large fuel and capacity penalties on Asia–Europe lanes.

2. **Panama Canal**
   Drought and transit limits compressed capacity on Americas–Asia routes, amplifying backlog risk when other corridors strain.

3. **Freight & capacity**
   Rerouting cuts effective global capacity and lifts rates; disruption compounds tariff-driven demand shifts.

**Bullets:**
- Contingency routing and multi-carrier contracts moved from “nice to have” to operating standard.
- Inventory buffers rose again: executives cite stock builds alongside diversification as top 2026 moves.

**Source note:**
Container News; industry disruption briefings 2025–26; DP World GTO 2026

## SLIDE 6 — Strategic Responses

**Section label:** 05 — Strategic responses

**Title:**
Four playbooks, one goal: optionality

**Cards:**

1. **China + 1 / + N** (Diversify)
   Keep China where it still wins on scale; add Vietnam, India, Mexico, and others for tariff and continuity hedges. Cited by ~51% of trade execs as a top 2026 shift.

2. **Friendshoring** (Align)
   ~36% plan deeper ties with politically aligned partners — useful, but alliance labels shift with policy.

3. **Nearshoring** (Regionalize)
   Mexico remains pivotal for US-bound goods, yet tariff uncertainty and USMCA risk have cooled confidence for many.

4. **Reshoring** (Rebuild)
   Formal reshoring plans jumped (to ~26% in KPMG survey), but most say full moves take 1–3 years amid labor, capital, and tier-2 gaps.

**Source note:**
DP World Global Trade Observatory 2026; Kearney Reshoring Index 2026; KPMG Tariff Survey 2026

**Key stats:**

| Stat | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 94% | of trade executives expect 2026 growth to match or beat 2025 |

- Digital visibility, supplier mapping, and FTA-aware routing are rising as growth enablers, not only as risk tools.

## SLIDE 8 — Implications for Leaders

**Section label:** 07 — Implications for leaders

**Title:**
Design for disruption, not for calm

**Bullets:**
- **Treat tariffs as a permanent planning variable** — model rate paths, carve-outs, and retaliation in every sourcing case.
- **Map multi-tier exposure** — assembly outside China still fails if critical inputs and tooling do not.
- **Invest in dual routes and dual suppliers** — chokepoint and conflict risk will keep capacity lumpy.
- **Prefer flexible capital** — smaller, staged bets beat large irreversible bets while policy stays fluid.
- **Make resilience a P&L story** — boards reward continuity that protects revenue, not only cost wins.

**Closing line:**
The winners of this cycle will not be the cheapest networks — they will be the ones that can reconfigure fastest when the next shock lands.

**Tagline:**
Resilience is now a growth strategy.
