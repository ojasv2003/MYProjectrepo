# Discussion Note  
## Network Optimisation and Middle-Mile Synergy between V-Trans and V-Xpress

| | |
|---|---|
| **Document type** | Internal discussion note |
| **Subject** | Network optimisation and synergy between V-Trans and V-Xpress |
| **Scope** | Middle-mile hub–branch structure, routing and feeder alignment |
| **Out of scope** | Need-based scheduling and spot vehicle engagement |

---

### 1. Purpose

This note sets out a structured approach to evaluate and unlock **network optimisation** and **middle-mile synergy** between V-Trans and V-Xpress—two operating verticals under the same legal entity. The intent is to improve asset utilisation, reduce duplication of hubs/branches and routes, and strengthen service economics without compromising the distinct commercial propositions of either business.

---

### 2. Background

V-Trans and V-Xpress operate under one legal entity but as distinct business verticals, each serving clients with transportation solutions of a different character.

| Dimension | V-Trans | V-Xpress |
|---|---|---|
| Business model | Conventional part-truck-load (PTL) operator | National express logistics provider |
| Geographic strength | Strongest in West; followed by South and North | National express footprint |
| Geographic weakness | East is the weakest zone | East is also the weakest zone |
| Client & service proposition | Differentiated on pricing, shipment profile, service quality and time sensitivity | Differentiated on pricing, shipment profile, service quality and time sensitivity |
| Middle-mile design | Independent hub–branch and line-haul construct | Independent hub–branch and line-haul construct |

While client propositions differ in pricing, shipment profile, service quality and time sensitivity, the **middle-mile operating models are entirely distinct**. At the same time, both businesses largely cover similar geographic territories. Differences are often limited to:

- choice of hub cities in selected markets; and  
- within the same city, different site locations rather than different catchments.

This overlap creates a clear opportunity for **structural synergy**, provided integration respects business-model and site-level constraints.

---

### 3. Objective

The organisation seeks to:

1. **Optimise the network and routing** of both businesses; and  
2. **Evaluate and capture synergy** in the middle-mile network of V-Trans and V-Xpress.

**Explicitly excluded from this project:** need-based scheduling and spot engagement. The focus is on the **structural network**—hubs, branches, fixed connections and feeder design—not tactical daily vehicle hiring.

---

### 4. Problem framing

The mandate comprises two related but non-identical problems:

| Problem | Description |
|---|---|
| **A. Network optimisation** | Redesigning hubs, branches, line-haul and feeder routes for efficiency, cost and service under projected combined (and residual standalone) loads |
| **B. Synergy evaluation** | Identifying where co-location, shared connections and shared feeder design create value without damaging either vertical’s service promise |

As of today, the two networks run **independently**. A full merger of middle-mile operations would yield benefits in principle; however, bringing both businesses onto the same physical sites and connections may be constrained by:

- business-model requirements (PTL vs express; handling, cut-offs, SLA);  
- site suitability (space, access, compliance, landlord terms); and  
- connection / lane design differences.

Consequently, **network and route optimisation cannot be run credibly in isolation**. Meaningful optimisation becomes possible only after hub and branch footprints are **synchronised and aligned wherever feasible**. Non-aligned nodes must be treated as residual standalone capacity in the final design.

---

### 5. Guiding principles

1. **Sequence before scale** — Align physical nodes first; optimise combined routes thereafter.  
2. **Synergy with selectivity** — Co-locate and combine only where operationally and commercially justified.  
3. **Preserve service identity** — Express and PTL propositions must not be diluted by forced integration.  
4. **Evidence before relocation** — Decisions on shifting sites must rest on load, connection and catchment analysis.  
5. **Exclude tactical noise** — Spot and ad-hoc scheduling remain outside the optimisation scope.  
6. **Phased execution** — Prefer staged, reversible moves over a big-bang network redesign.

---

### 6. Recommended phased approach

All project activities cannot—and should not—run concurrently. A step-wise programme is proposed as follows.

#### Phase 1 — Hub and branch alignment assessment

**Objective:** Establish a fact base of current locations, loads and connections for both entities, and identify where co-location / alignment is feasible.

**Key activities**

- Map all hubs and branches of V-Trans and V-Xpress independently (city, site, role, capacity).  
- Map loads and middle-mile connections for each node.  
- Overlay both networks to identify:
  - **Low-hanging opportunities** — same city / nearby sites; quick co-location possible;  
  - **Medium-term opportunities** — alignment possible after lease, layout or process changes;  
  - **Non-doable locations** — constrained by business model, site, or connection design.  
- Where relevant, identify preferred branch locations using **centre-of-gravity** analysis of pick-up and delivery volumes.

**Output:** Alignment opportunity matrix (doable / deferred / not feasible), with indicative benefit and constraint notes by location.

---

#### Phase 2 — Hub and branch alignment execution (interim window)

**Objective:** Physically align selected sites—especially short-term, low-hanging opportunities—before a full network optimisation is run.

**Key activities**

- Execute co-location / site shifts for prioritised locations.  
- Stabilise operations at aligned sites (cut-offs, handling norms, space allocation between PTL and express where shared).  
- Maintain a live register of aligned vs non-aligned nodes.

**Indicative duration:** approximately **3 to 6 months**, depending on lease cycles and execution complexity.

**Rationale:** Running a combined network model on a footprint that is still in flux will produce unstable and non-implementable recommendations. An intermediate alignment window improves the quality of the subsequent optimisation.

---

#### Phase 3 — Combined middle-mile network optimisation

**Objective:** Optimise the middle-mile network on the **combined load** of both businesses, once doable and non-doable hub alignments are clearly visible.

**Key activities**

- Build a combined demand and connection view for aligned geography.  
- Optimise hub roles, line-haul routes and frequencies for the joint load.  
- Treat **non-aligned V-Xpress (and/or V-Trans) nodes separately** in the final design so that residual standalone requirements are not forced into an invalid combined solution.  
- Stress-test service implications (transit time, cut-offs, handovers) for both verticals.

**Output:** Recommended middle-mile network design—combined where aligned; standalone overlays where not.

---

#### Phase 4 — Feeder route alignment and optimisation

**Objective:** As the final structural step, align and optimise feeder routes feeding the (re)designed hub–branch network.

**Key activities**

- Redesign feeder circuits around aligned hubs/branches.  
- Eliminate duplicate feeder coverage where catchments overlap.  
- Confirm first-mile / last-mile interface impacts on service quality for both businesses.

**Output:** Feeder plan consistent with the optimised middle-mile backbone.

---

### 7. High-level roadmap

| Stage | Focus | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Assess** | Independent mapping + co-location opportunity matrix | Clear list of doable / deferred / non-doable alignments |
| **2. Align** | 3–6 month execution of short-term site alignments | Stabilised joint footprint for optimisation |
| **3. Optimise** | Combined middle-mile network design; residual nodes handled separately | Implementable joint + standalone network blueprint |
| **4. Extend** | Feeder route alignment and optimisation | End-to-end structural network synergy |

---

### 8. Proposed methodology and tools

| Option | Suitability for this programme |
|---|---|
| Network design tools (e.g. Log-hub, LLamasoft / Coupa, IBM ILOG-class solvers) | Useful for refinement at a later stage once the aligned footprint and data model are stable |
| Structured manual / Excel-based analysis | **Recommended for the first implementation cycle**, given current dual-network complexity, co-location constraints and the need for judgement on business-model fit |

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **manual, analytically structured approach** for Phase 1–3 in the first wave. Tool-based optimisation may be explored subsequently to improve precision and scenario testing once alignment decisions and data quality support a model-driven exercise.

---

### 9. Data and analytical requirements (indicative)

To execute the approach, the programme will require (at minimum):

- Hub/branch master with city, site coordinates, role, capacity and lease/ownership status (both entities);  
- Middle-mile lane and connection matrix (origin–destination, frequency, vehicle type, utilisation);  
- Volume and shipment-profile data by node and lane (PTL vs express);  
- Service parameters (cut-offs, target transit, handling constraints);  
- Cost elements relevant to line-haul, hub handling and facility duplication;  
- City-level pick-up/delivery density for centre-of-gravity branch assessment.

---

### 10. Expected benefits (directional)

Subject to validation in Phases 1–3, the programme is expected to enable:

- reduction in duplicate facility and connection cost in overlapping cities;  
- improved middle-mile vehicle utilisation on shared corridors;  
- clearer hub roles and fewer fragmented line-haul runs;  
- stronger East-zone design options through joint volume visibility (a shared weakness today);  
- a cleaner base for future tool-based optimisation.

Benefits should be quantified location-by-location during the alignment assessment and reconfirmed after the combined optimisation.

---

### 11. Risks and constraints to manage

| Risk / constraint | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Forced co-location harms express or PTL service identity | Apply selective synergy; retain standalone design where needed |
| Lease and site constraints delay alignment | Prioritise low-hanging sites; park non-doables explicitly |
| Optimising before alignment produces non-implementable plans | Enforce the Phase 1 → 2 → 3 sequence |
| Incomplete or inconsistent dual-network data | Invest in mapping quality before optimisation |
| Scope creep into spot / daily scheduling | Keep tactical hiring outside project boundaries |

---

### 12. Decision points for leadership

Leadership guidance is sought on the following:

1. **Acceptance of the phased sequence** (assess → align → optimise → feeder), rather than a single-step joint optimisation.  
2. **Mandate to execute short-term co-locations** during the 3–6 month interim window where the opportunity matrix supports it.  
3. **Confirmation that spot/scheduling optimisation remains out of scope** for this project.  
4. **Agreement to commence with a manual analytical methodology**, with tool-based enhancement as a subsequent phase.  
5. **Nomination of a joint steering group** (Operations / Network from both verticals + Finance) to approve doable / non-doable classifications.

---

### 13. Conclusion

V-Trans and V-Xpress share geography more than they share middle-mile design. That overlap is the opportunity; the difference in business model is the constraint. The correct response is not an immediate full merger of networks, nor independent optimisation of two siloed systems. It is a **disciplined, phased programme**: align hubs and branches where feasible, execute the practicable co-locations, then optimise the combined middle-mile—and finally feeders—while explicitly carrying non-aligned nodes as residual design.

This sequence maximises synergy where it is real, protects service propositions where integration is not viable, and produces a network plan that can actually be implemented.
