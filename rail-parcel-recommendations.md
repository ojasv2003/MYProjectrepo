# Recommendations: Productising Rail Parcel as an Ecosystem-Led Offering

## Strategic posture

The next leap in rail parcel requires a different posture: treat parcel as a **productised, ecosystem-led logistics offering**, not as residual capacity attached to passenger trains. Historical growth in the segment has come largely through piecemeal upgrades; what is now required is transformative change.

Railways should sharpen focus where it already creates unmatched value—**network control and middle-mile connectivity**—and partner for non-core capabilities such as first-mile booking, last-mile delivery, and specialised technology. In this model, Railways has the potential to orchestrate the broader parcel ecosystem around the spine of its network and middle-mile strengths. Commercially, customer-facing logistics work can be separated from the rail spine, while handoffs remain crisp at dedicated parcel interfaces.

## Recommended operating model: hub-and-spoke with clear roles

The network design should mirror express-cargo practice: concentrate volume at hubs and move **hub-to-hub with zero intermediate touchpoints**. Spoke and last-mile legs can be undertaken by logistics service providers over the road network.

Roles and accountability of each participant should be defined clearly, along lines similar to air cargo:

| Participant | Recommended accountability |
|---|---|
| **Logistics Service Operator / Provider** | Owns the customer interface, booking, consolidation and deconsolidation centres, and final delivery. |
| **Terminal Operator** | Manages connected rail-head operations—segregation, unitisation, loading/unloading, receiving, and transshipment. |
| **Rail Network** | Provides solely middle-mile connectivity using parcel trains / VPU / SLR / customised consists, including roll-cage operations for SLR. |

## Dedicated parcel terminals

Dedicated parcel terminals will be required at key cities aligned to industrial and consumption clusters. These hubs should be located **outside cluttered city constraints**, yet remain convenient for B2B shippers and industry—the segments that contribute the highest volumes of parcels and part-load traffic.

## Expected outcome

Shared digital rails, clean commercial interfaces, and hub-and-spoke physical design together have the potential to convert today’s fragmentation into a competitive **door-to-door** parcel product.
