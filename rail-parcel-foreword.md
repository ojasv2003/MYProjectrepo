# Foreword

Indian Railways has long been the backbone of the nation’s movement of people and bulk commodities. That foundation remains indispensable. What has changed is the character of demand around it. E-commerce, organised retail, manufacturing, and a growing base of MSMEs are reshaping logistics around smaller consignments, tighter timelines, and door-to-door reliability. In this shift, the middle mile—long-haul movement between major markets—has become the decisive lever of cost, capacity, and carbon performance. It is also where the railway’s comparative advantage is strongest.

Yet that advantage has not fully translated into the parcel and express segment. Rail parcel volumes and revenues, while meaningful and growing, remain a thin slice of overall freight and an even thinner share of India’s long-haul part-load and express market. Road continues to dominate because of flexibility and first- and last-mile reach; air retains a premium niche for time-critical cargo. The opportunity for rail is not to imitate either mode, but to occupy the middle mile with scheduled, high-density, hub-to-hub services that private logistics partners can extend to the customer’s door.

This report examines that opportunity in full. It sizes the addressable and serviceable markets for rail parcel, reviews how mature ecosystems in Europe and China organise collaboration between railway operators and courier–express–parcel players, and diagnoses the structural constraints that still hold back Indian Railways’ parcel business—from terminal design and digitisation to containerisation, commercial models, and organisational focus. It also documents the policy and product initiatives already underway, including leasing reforms, Parcel Cargo Express Trains, Jan Sadharan Parcel services, Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals, and emerging digital tracking capabilities.

The central conclusion is clear: incremental improvements will not be enough. Parcel must be treated as a productised, ecosystem-led offering rather than residual capacity attached to passenger operations. Railways should own and strengthen the network spine and middle-mile connectivity, while logistics service providers and terminal operators take clear accountability for customer interface, consolidation, and last-mile delivery. Shared digital systems, standardised handling units, and dedicated parcel hubs aligned to industrial and consumption clusters are the practical foundations of that model.

If Indian Railways can make this transition, rail parcel can move from a peripheral revenue line to a competitive, scalable, and sustainable logistics product—one that serves traders, farmers, MSMEs, and modern retail alike, and that converts national network depth into a genuine growth engine for the country’s express and part-load economy.

We commend this report to policymakers, railway leadership, logistics operators, and industry partners as a shared agenda for that transformation.
