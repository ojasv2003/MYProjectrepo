FOREWORD

Logistics is the quiet architecture of a growing economy. Every product that moves from a factory floor, a farm gate or a fulfilment centre to a distant market depends on a chain of decisions about cost, time, reliability and environmental impact. For more than a century, Indian Railways has carried that responsibility at national scale—binding regions together and moving bulk commodities with an efficiency few other modes can match. The question this report places before policymakers, railway leadership, logistics operators and industry partners is whether that same national asset can now become the backbone of India’s next logistics chapter: modern parcel and express cargo.

The timing could not be more consequential. E-commerce, organised retail, manufacturing supply chains and MSME trade are generating steady growth in smaller, time-sensitive consignments. Road transport still dominates this space because of flexibility and door-to-door reach. Yet the middle mile—the long haul between cities and clusters—is precisely where rail’s network depth, energy efficiency and capacity advantage are strongest. Capturing that opportunity is not merely a commercial ambition for the Railways; it is a national logistics and sustainability priority.

This report has been prepared to bring clarity to that opportunity. It assesses the present scale of rail parcel services, sizes the addressable and serviceable markets, and draws practical lessons from European and Chinese railway parcel ecosystems. It is equally candid about the gaps that hold India back: infrastructure designed around passenger stations, limited mechanisation and digitisation, fragmented handling, and an operating culture that has often treated parcel as residual capacity rather than a productised logistics offering.

Importantly, the report does not stop at diagnosis. It reviews the initiatives already underway—leasing reforms, Parcel Cargo Express Trains, Jan Sadharan Parcel services, Gati Shakti terminals, and digital visibility efforts—and then outlines a way forward grounded in a collaborative operating model. The central idea is simple and powerful: let the Railways concentrate on what they do best—network control and reliable middle-mile movement—while partners deliver customer interface, consolidation and last-mile reach. With dedicated hubs, clean commercial interfaces and shared digital systems, fragmentation can give way to a coherent door-to-door proposition.

I hope this document serves as both a mirror and a map: a mirror that reflects where the rail parcel ecosystem stands today, and a map that guides the transformative steps required to unlock its full potential. The opportunity is large; the foundations exist; what remains is purposeful execution. May this report contribute usefully to that journey.
